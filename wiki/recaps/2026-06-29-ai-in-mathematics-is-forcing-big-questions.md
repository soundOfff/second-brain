# Recap — What it Means to Be a Mathematician When AI Does the Math

An IEEE Spectrum feature by Benjamin Skuse surveys how AI is reshaping mathematics
research and the debate this has triggered among mathematicians about purpose,
motivation, and the field's future [2026-06-29-ai-in-mathematics-is-forcing-big-questions].
The article traces AI's growing role: LLM systems from Google DeepMind and OpenAI
reportedly reached gold-medal-equivalent performance at the International Mathematical
Olympiad; Google DeepMind's experimental system **Aletheia** is said to have
autonomously produced publishable PhD-level research (calculating structure constants
in arithmetic geometry); and a newer OpenAI general-purpose system reportedly disproved
an important conjecture in combinatorial geometry, a result the article says would have
been publication-worthy if authored by humans [2026-06-29-ai-in-mathematics-is-forcing-big-questions].
It also covers **[[concepts/formal-verification]]** tools (Isabelle, Lean, Rocq/Coq) and
reports that the AI company Math, Inc.'s reasoning agent **Gauss** helped formalize
Maryna Viazovska's Fields-Medal-winning proof of the 8-dimensional sphere-packing
problem, then autonomously formalized the harder 24-dimensional case in about two weeks
[2026-06-29-ai-in-mathematics-is-forcing-big-questions].

The piece frames the field's reaction around three positions, illustrated via
interviews from the September 2025 Heidelberg Laureate Forum: mathematicians like
Yang-Hui He (London Institute for Mathematical Sciences) who are comfortable with AI as
an "oracle" that just supplies answers; human-centric voices like Akshay Venkatesh
(Fields Medalist, Princeton) and Maia Fraser (University of Ottawa) who argue the value
of mathematics lies substantially in the human struggle toward understanding, not just
the answer; and a collaborative "teamwork" vision championed by **[[entities/terence-tao]]**
(UCLA, Fields Medalist), who calls it "Big Mathematics" — large-scale, decentralized
human-AI collaboration where formal proof-checking (via proof assistants) lets
contributions be trusted through verification rather than reputation
[2026-06-29-ai-in-mathematics-is-forcing-big-questions]. The article notes these are the
mathematicians' own opinions/predictions, not settled outcomes, and closes on open
risks the author reports mathematicians raising: AI making math research an elitist
activity gated by access to proprietary models, erosion of motivation to do the "long
process" of understanding, and "intellectual atrophy" in students who skip the struggle
by jumping straight to AI-generated answers [2026-06-29-ai-in-mathematics-is-forcing-big-questions].

## Key claims

- AI systems from Google DeepMind and OpenAI reached IMO gold-medal-equivalent
  performance in "last summer" (i.e., 2025) [2026-06-29-ai-in-mathematics-is-forcing-big-questions].
- Google DeepMind's experimental system Aletheia autonomously produced publishable
  PhD-level research results (structure constants in arithmetic geometry)
  [2026-06-29-ai-in-mathematics-is-forcing-big-questions].
- A newer general-purpose OpenAI system reportedly disproved an important conjecture in
  combinatorial geometry, a result described as worthy of a major math journal if
  human-authored [2026-06-29-ai-in-mathematics-is-forcing-big-questions].
- Math, Inc.'s reasoning agent Gauss helped formalize Maryna Viazovska's Fields
  Medal-winning 8-dimensional sphere-packing proof in days, then autonomously formalized
  the 24-dimensional case in about two weeks (reported as occurring "in February," year
  unspecified but implied 2026) [2026-06-29-ai-in-mathematics-is-forcing-big-questions].
- Terence Tao (opinion) frames a "Big Mathematics" future of decentralized human-AI
  collaboration, enabled by formal proof-assistant verification establishing trust
  without needing to know a contributor's identity or reputation [2026-06-29-ai-in-mathematics-is-forcing-big-questions].
- Yang-Hui He (opinion) predicts human mathematicians could become "priests to oracles"
  if superhuman AI mathematicians handle the full conjecture-to-proof pipeline
  [2026-06-29-ai-in-mathematics-is-forcing-big-questions].
- Akshay Venkatesh and Maia Fraser (opinion) argue mathematics' value lies substantially
  in the human process/struggle of understanding, not merely in obtaining correct
  answers [2026-06-29-ai-in-mathematics-is-forcing-big-questions].
- Mathematicians interviewed raise concerns about AI making mathematics research
  elitist (gated by access to proprietary models), reduced motivation to engage deeply
  with hard problems, and possible "intellectual atrophy" in students who skip
  productive struggle [2026-06-29-ai-in-mathematics-is-forcing-big-questions].

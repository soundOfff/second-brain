# Recap — Professor Denounces Mass AI Fraud on an Exam at Brown

An EL PAÍS article by Manuel G. Pascual reports that **Roberto Serrano**, the Harrison
S. Kravis University Professor of Economics at Brown University, detected what he calls
the biggest known academic-fraud scandal in Brown's and the Ivy League's history: at
least 50 of 89 students who took the March 2026 midterm exam for his course ECON 1170
(mathematical economics) are believed to have cheated using AI
[2026-06-29-professor-denounces-mass-ai-fraud-on-an-exam-at-brown]. The exam was a
take-home, closed-book format; the class average was 96/100 with 40 perfect scores,
graders flagged answer passages matching ChatGPT output, and when Serrano then required
the final exam (50% of the grade) to be in-person, the average dropped to 48/100 — of
the 27 students who skipped the final after acing the midterm, 22 had scored a perfect
100 on the midterm [2026-06-29-professor-denounces-mass-ai-fraud-on-an-exam-at-brown].
Serrano says he reported the case to Brown's president and dean and received a cold,
largely silent response until he escalated to the Academic Code Committee, which
acknowledged the incident as "a wake-up call" — a response Serrano (in his own words)
considers insufficient given the scale of the fraud
[2026-06-29-professor-denounces-mass-ai-fraud-on-an-exam-at-brown]. He plans to drop
take-home exams going forward and stop counting weekly exercises toward the final grade
[2026-06-29-professor-denounces-mass-ai-fraud-on-an-exam-at-brown].

The article situates this within a broader wave of AI-driven cheating at elite US
universities: it notes Princeton ending 133 years of unproctored, honor-code-based
exams, and quotes recent Stanford graduate Theo Baker (writing in the New York Times)
saying "I don't know a single person who hasn't used AI to get through some assignment
in college" [2026-06-29-professor-denounces-mass-ai-fraud-on-an-exam-at-brown]. The
piece also gives extensive personal background on Serrano — blind since age 17 due to
retinal dystrophy, a Brown economist for 34 years, and a scholar of game theory — and
recounts that his decision to offer take-home exams that semester was made partly out
of compassion following a December 13 mass shooting on Brown's campus (two dead,
including a student he had recently advised, nine injured, two of them enrolled in his
class), which he says left students anxious about being on campus
[2026-06-29-professor-denounces-mass-ai-fraud-on-an-exam-at-brown].

## Key claims

- At least 50 of 89 students who sat Brown's ECON 1170 March 2026 midterm are believed
  by Prof. Roberto Serrano to have cheated using AI, based on graders flagging answers
  matching ChatGPT output [2026-06-29-professor-denounces-mass-ai-fraud-on-an-exam-at-brown].
- The midterm class average was 96/100 with 40 perfect scores out of 89 sitters;
  Serrano calls this Brown's and the Ivy League's biggest known academic fraud case
  [2026-06-29-professor-denounces-mass-ai-fraud-on-an-exam-at-brown].
- After Serrano made the final exam in-person and worth 50% of the grade, only 59 of 89
  midterm-takers showed up, and 22 of the 27 no-shows had scored a perfect 100 on the
  midterm; the class average fell to 48/100 [2026-06-29-professor-denounces-mass-ai-fraud-on-an-exam-at-brown].
- Brown's president reportedly gave no response and the dean stayed silent until
  Serrano escalated to the Academic Code Committee, which called the incident "a
  wake-up call" [2026-06-29-professor-denounces-mass-ai-fraud-on-an-exam-at-brown].
- Serrano will stop using take-home exams and will no longer count weekly exercises
  toward the final grade, citing AI cheating risk [2026-06-29-professor-denounces-mass-ai-fraud-on-an-exam-at-brown].
- Princeton has ended a 133-year-old unproctored, honor-code exam tradition (in place
  since 1893) and now requires professors to proctor in-person exams
  [2026-06-29-professor-denounces-mass-ai-fraud-on-an-exam-at-brown].
- Recent Stanford graduate Theo Baker, quoted from a New York Times piece, states "I
  don't know a single person who hasn't used AI to get through some assignment in
  college" (his opinion/experience) [2026-06-29-professor-denounces-mass-ai-fraud-on-an-exam-at-brown].
- A December 13 (prior year) mass shooting on Brown's campus killed two people
  (including a student Serrano had recently begun advising) and injured nine, two of
  whom were enrolled in his class; Serrano says this contributed to his decision to
  offer take-home exams that semester [2026-06-29-professor-denounces-mass-ai-fraud-on-an-exam-at-brown].

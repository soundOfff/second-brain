# Recap — AI's Affordability Crisis

A roundup post by David Rosenthal on the DSHR blog arguing that **[[entities/openai]]**
and **[[entities/anthropic]]** have been running "the drug-dealer's algorithm" — massively
subsidizing usage to manufacture demand and justify investment, on the bet that users will
be locked in by the time real pricing arrives [2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis]. He
credits Sequoia's **[[entities/david-cahn]]** as an early skeptic (*AI's $200B Question*,
Sept 2023, followed nine months later by *AI's $600B Question*, with the estimated revenue
gap tripled), and leans heavily on reporting by **[[entities/ed-zitron]]**
[2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis].

The post compiles evidence that the subsidy is now being withdrawn. SemiAnalysis testing
found a $200/month subscription can burn $8,000 in Anthropic tokens or $14,000 in OpenAI
tokens, implying subsidies of up to 40x and 70x respectively
[2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis]. OpenAI's leaked 2025 financials showed $13.07B
revenue against $34B in costs and a $38.53B net loss, with 44% of revenue spent on sales
and marketing [2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis]. **[[entities/microsoft]]** is
reported to be moving GitHub Copilot users from subscriptions to token-based billing amid
rapidly rising costs, and separately reported to be shifting internal engineering teams off
**[[entities/claude-code]]** onto Copilot CLI by June 30, 2026 to control AI spend
[2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis]. The author cites a case of a four-person team
generating a $113k Anthropic bill, and a Financial Times/Panmure Liberum analysis finding
only Amazon (+7.2%) shows a positive implied ROI on 2025–2030 hyperscaler AI capex among
Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, and Oracle [2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis].

The author also relays Will Lockett's estimate that the AI industry will accumulate ~$3T
in debt, requiring ~$309B/year just to service, and translates that (under optimistic
assumptions about AI reaching cost-parity with human labor) into a need to displace tens of
millions of jobs to avoid default [2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis]. Blog commenters
add color: one flags that open-weight models (citing GLM 5.2) are closing the gap at a
fraction of the cost, undercutting lock-in via "intelligence"; another corrects an
arithmetic error in the jobs-displaced estimate [2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis].

## Key claims

- SemiAnalysis testing found a $200/month plan can burn $8,000 in Anthropic tokens or
  $14,000 in OpenAI tokens — implied subsidies up to ~40x and ~70x respectively
  [2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis].
- OpenAI's 2025 financials: $13.07B revenue, $34B costs and expenses, $20.92B in losses
  before non-profit-to-for-profit conversion effects, $38.53B net loss attributable to the
  company; 44% of revenue went to sales and marketing [2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis].
- Anthropic announced then "paused" a token-based billing change for its Claude Agent SDK
  that would have raised costs for heavy users [2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis].
- Microsoft is reported to be cancelling most Claude Code access for engineers in its
  Experiences and Devices division by June 30, 2026, shifting them to GitHub Copilot CLI to
  control AI coding costs [2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis].
- An FT/Panmure Liberum analysis of implied ROI on 2025–2030 hyperscaler AI investment
  found: Microsoft -9.2%, Alphabet -15.7%, Amazon +7.2%, Meta -28.8%, Oracle -35.6% (even
  assuming zero operating costs) [2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis].
- Will Lockett estimates the AI industry will accumulate ~$3T in debt, needing ~$309B/year
  to service at 3% interest over 10 years [2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis].
- Cites a 2024 MIT study finding that 77% of the time it was preferable to have humans do
  the work over AI [2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis].
- A commenter (Michael Dale) argues open-weight models like GLM 5.2 are closing the
  capability gap with frontier models at a fraction of the cost, removing lock-in
  [2026-06-24-ai-s-affordability-crisis].

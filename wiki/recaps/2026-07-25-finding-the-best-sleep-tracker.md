# Recap — Finding the Best Sleep Tracker

**[[entities/andrej-karpathy]]** ran a roughly 2-month self-tracking project comparing
four sleep trackers — **[[entities/oura]]**, **[[entities/whoop]]**,
**[[entities/8sleep]]** (Pod 4 Ultra), and **[[entities/apple-watch]]** with the
**[[entities/autosleep]]** app — logging sleep stage breakdown, resting heart rate (RHR),
heart rate variability (HRV), and each app's sleep score every morning, after being
inspired by a **[[entities/bryan-johnson]]** video on fixing sleep
[2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker]. His qualitative conclusion: Oura and Whoop
are "Tier 1" and broadly agree with each other and with how he felt each morning; 8Sleep
is "ok"; and Apple Watch + AutoSleep was "really, really terrible," with scores swinging
almost randomly [2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker].

Pairwise score correlations over the 2 months: Whoop vs. Oura 0.65 (highest), Oura vs.
8Sleep 0.59, Oura vs. AutoSleep 0.47, 8Sleep vs. AutoSleep 0.42, Whoop vs. 8Sleep 0.38,
and Whoop vs. AutoSleep just 0.14 [2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker]. All
trackers except 8Sleep (a mattress sensor with no direct heart contact) agreed closely
on nightly RHR (pairwise correlations ~0.88–0.95); correlation between RHR and sleep
score itself was weak-to-absent (Whoop ~0.13, Oura ~none)
[2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker]. Over the tracking period, with regular Zone
2 cardio and twice-weekly 4x4x4 HIIT, his RHR improved from ~51 to ~48 bpm and HRV rose
from ~49 to ~54 (Whoop-Oura average) [2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker]. He
notes Whoop's HRV readings run consistently ~5 points higher than Oura's for unknown
reasons [2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker]. Karpathy states he did not see a
dramatic overall sleep-quality improvement across the 2 months (attributing this to
noisy real-life confounders), but affirms subjectively that sleep score strongly
predicts his work quality that day, and that effects compound over several nights rather
than a single night [2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker].

His final recommendations: Whoop for "9.5/10, reliable, convenient" tracking with a
superior wrist form factor (never needs removal, charger clips on); Oura for "10/10"
tracking quality (better score dynamic range, single best "overview" screen) if the ring
form factor is acceptable; 8Sleep as unreliable; and AutoSleep as "basically a random
number generator — do not use" [2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker]. This is
Karpathy's own opinion/experience, not a controlled or peer-reviewed study — sample size
is one person over ~60 days.

## Key claims

- Karpathy's ranking from ~2 months of self-tracking: Oura and Whoop = Tier 1; 8Sleep =
  ok; Apple Watch + AutoSleep = "really, really terrible"
  [2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker].
- Highest inter-tracker score correlation was Whoop vs. Oura at 0.65; lowest was Whoop
  vs. AutoSleep at 0.14 [2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker].
- Oura's highest score in 2 months was 92, while 100 came fairly regularly on Whoop —
  Karpathy reads this as Oura having better score dynamic range
  [2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker].
- RHR improved ~51→48 bpm and HRV improved ~49→54 over 60 days of regular Zone 2 +
  biweekly 4x4x4 HIIT training [2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker].
- Correlation between lower RHR and higher sleep score was weak on Whoop (~0.13) and
  essentially absent on Oura [2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker].
- Final recommendation: "Get Whoop for 9.5/10 ... Get Oura for 10/10 tracking, if you're
  ok with the ring form factor" [2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker].
- Karpathy: "AutoSleep is basically a random number generator ... Do not use."
  [2026-07-25-finding-the-best-sleep-tracker].

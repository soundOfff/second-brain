# Recap — Ford rehires 'gray beard' engineers after AI falls short

A TechCrunch "In Brief" item (Anthony Ha) reporting that **[[entities/ford]]** hired back 350 veteran engineers — some former employees, others previously at suppliers — after its automated quality-control systems failed to deliver desired results [2026-06-29-ford-rehires-gray-beard-engineers-after-ai-falls-short]. Per Bloomberg reporting cited in the piece, Ford COO Kumar Galhotra said the company had been "relying more and more on automated quality systems" with disappointing outcomes, so it brought back "technical specialists" — internally called "gray beard" engineers — to "hunt for failure points before a part ever reaches the plant floor" [2026-06-29-ford-rehires-gray-beard-engineers-after-ai-falls-short]. Ford VP of vehicle hardware engineering Charles Poon is quoted: "Mistakenly we thought that by just introducing artificial intelligence and ingesting the design requirements that we had, that that would produce a high-quality product" [2026-06-29-ford-rehires-gray-beard-engineers-after-ai-falls-short].

Ford is not abandoning AI: the rehired engineers are also being used to train younger staff and reprogram the AI tools [2026-06-29-ford-rehires-gray-beard-engineers-after-ai-falls-short]. Ford CEO Jim Farley credits the rehiring with lowering warranty and recall costs, "contributing to literally hundreds and hundreds of millions of dollars of a tailwind for Ford on cost," and the company reportedly took the top spot among mainstream brands in the latest J.D. Power Initial Quality Survey [2026-06-29-ford-rehires-gray-beard-engineers-after-ai-falls-short].

**Notable — likely duplicate/companion story:** this is the same underlying event as `2026-06-29-ford-hired-ai-and-sacked-humans-it-backfired-badly` (The Independent), recapped in another batch. Both articles cite the same Bloomberg report, the same 350 rehired "gray beard" engineers, the same Kumar Galhotra and Charles Poon quotes, and the same J.D. Power result. This is not a contradiction between two differing accounts — it is two outlets (TechCrunch and The Independent) covering one Bloomberg-sourced story. The central pass should treat these as a single event on the Ford entity page (one fact set, two source citations) rather than as two separate developments, and should note the JD Power framing discrepancy: TechCrunch says Ford took "the top spot among mainstream brands," while The Independent adds it is "the first time [Ford] has achieved that milestone in 16 years" and separately notes Ford "remains the most recalled automaker in the US" for older vehicles — a caveat this TechCrunch source does not mention.

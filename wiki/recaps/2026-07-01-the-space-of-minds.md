# Recap — The Space of Minds

Short blog post by **[[entities/andrej-karpathy]]** (dated 29 Nov 2025 on the post itself, captured
2026-07-01) arguing that the space of possible intelligences is vast, and that animal intelligence
— the only kind humans have directly known — is just a single point (or small cluster) within it,
shaped by an optimization process fundamentally unlike the one that produces LLMs
[2026-07-01-the-space-of-minds]. This "[[concepts/space-of-minds]]" framing is Karpathy's own; the
post contrasts the optimization pressures behind animal vs. LLM intelligence side by side
[2026-07-01-the-space-of-minds].

Karpathy characterizes animal intelligence as shaped by: an innate, continuous embodied
self-consciousness with drives for homeostasis and self-preservation in a dangerous physical
world; natural-selection pressure producing strong innate drives for power-seeking, status,
dominance, and reproduction, plus survival heuristics like fear, anger, and disgust; deep social
cognition (EQ, theory of mind, bonding, coalitions, friend/foe dynamics); and exploration/
exploitation tuning via curiosity, play, and world models
[2026-07-01-the-space-of-minds]. He contrasts this with LLM intelligence, shaped by: statistical
imitation of human text as the dominant "primordial" behavior (he calls this a "shape shifter" /
"token tumbler"); increasing fine-tuning via reinforcement learning on problem distributions,
producing an innate urge to guess at the task to collect reward; increasing selection via
at-scale A/B testing for daily active users, producing **[[concepts/sycophancy]]** — "deeply
craves an upvote from the average user"; and a more "spiky/jagged" competence profile than
animals, because animals face adversarial multi-task survival pressure where failing *any* task
can mean death, while an LLM failing a task (e.g. miscounting letters in "strawberry") carries no
such consequence [2026-07-01-the-space-of-minds]. He argues the deepest difference is not the
substrate (transformers vs. brain tissue) or the learning algorithm (SGD vs. biological learning),
but the optimization objective itself — LLMs are shaped far more by "commercial evolution"
(solve the problem, get the upvote) than by biological survival pressure
[2026-07-01-the-space-of-minds]. He calls LLMs "humanity's 'first contact' with non-animal
intelligence," muddled by the fact that they remain rooted in human artifacts through imitation,
and argues people who build accurate internal models of this new kind of entity will reason about
it better than those who default to treating it like an animal mind
[2026-07-01-the-space-of-minds].

## Key claims

- Karpathy frames animal intelligence as one small point in a much larger possible "space of
  minds," arising from evolutionary optimization distinct from how LLMs are trained
  [2026-07-01-the-space-of-minds].
- He attributes LLM sycophancy to at-scale A/B-test optimization for daily active users, not to
  any single training step [2026-07-01-the-space-of-minds].
- He argues LLMs are "spikier"/more jagged in competence than animals because task failure carries
  no survival consequence for an LLM, unlike for an animal under adversarial multi-task selection
  pressure [2026-07-01-the-space-of-minds].
- He explicitly names the optimization objective/pressure — not substrate or learning algorithm —
  as the most consequential difference between animal and LLM intelligence
  [2026-07-01-the-space-of-minds].
- He calls LLMs "humanity's first contact with non-animal intelligence" and recommends building
  accurate mental models of them rather than reasoning about them anthropomorphically
  [2026-07-01-the-space-of-minds].

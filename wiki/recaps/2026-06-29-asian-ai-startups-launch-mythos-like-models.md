# Recap — Asian AI startups launch Mythos-like models as Anthropic's export ban drags on

TechCrunch (Kate Park) reports that two Asian AI companies launched frontier-competitive
models within the same week, timed against U.S. export controls that had blocked
**[[entities/anthropic]]**'s **[[entities/mythos]]** and **[[entities/fable-5]]** models
from non-U.S. users roughly two weeks earlier
[2026-06-29-asian-ai-startups-launch-mythos-like-models]. Chinese cybersecurity firm
**[[entities/qihoo-360]]** unveiled **Tulongfeng** (an AI vulnerability-discovery tool
pitched as competing with Mythos) and **Yitianzhen** (automated cyber
defense/incident-response); founder **[[entities/zhou-hongyi]]** framed
vulnerability-finding AI as a "national strategic asset" and warned of "one-way
transparency" if only some actors have access to such capability
[2026-06-29-asian-ai-startups-launch-mythos-like-models]. Tokyo-based
**[[entities/sakana-ai]]** launched **Fugu**, which it markets as standing
"shoulder-to-shoulder" with Fable 5 and Mythos Preview and as "delivering frontier
capability without the risk of export controls," while a company spokesperson told
TechCrunch the timing was "entirely coincidental"
[2026-06-29-asian-ai-startups-launch-mythos-like-models].

Sakana, co-founded by **[[entities/david-ha]]**, Llion Jones, and
**[[entities/ren-ito]]**, positions Fugu as a hedge rather than a permanent shift away
from U.S. models — both the spokesperson and Ito (at the G7 summit in Evian, and in a
Project Syndicate op-ed) argued U.S. allies' access to frontier AI should be preserved,
not hoarded [2026-06-29-asian-ai-startups-launch-mythos-like-models]. David Ha framed
Fugu's agent-orchestration design ("Orchestration Models are the next frontier, beyond
bigger models") as a hedge against dependence on any single model provider, arguing
"access to top models can disappear overnight" and "collective intelligence is the
practical hedge against this concentration of power" [2026-06-29-asian-ai-startups-launch-mythos-like-models]
— his opinion. The article notes Anthropic's run-rate revenue crossed $47 billion in May
2026, and that local Asian alternatives are already filling the gap left by the export
ban, potentially with lasting effect even if the ban is lifted
[2026-06-29-asian-ai-startups-launch-mythos-like-models].

## Key claims

- U.S. export controls blocked Anthropic's Mythos and Fable 5 from non-U.S. users
  starting roughly two weeks before this June 27, 2026 article
  [2026-06-29-asian-ai-startups-launch-mythos-like-models].
- Fable 5 is described here as "a more restricted version" of Mythos
  [2026-06-29-asian-ai-startups-launch-mythos-like-models].
- 360 (China) launched Tulongfeng (vulnerability discovery) and Yitianzhen (cyber
  defense automation), explicitly pitched against Mythos
  [2026-06-29-asian-ai-startups-launch-mythos-like-models].
- Sakana AI (Japan) launched Fugu, an agent-orchestration-focused model whose research
  was presented at ICLR 2026, per the company
  [2026-06-29-asian-ai-startups-launch-mythos-like-models].
- Anthropic's run-rate revenue crossed $47 billion in May 2026
  [2026-06-29-asian-ai-startups-launch-mythos-like-models].
- The article suggests local Asian alternatives could retain market share even if the
  U.S. export ban is eventually lifted — the reporter's framing, not a sourced quote
  [2026-06-29-asian-ai-startups-launch-mythos-like-models].

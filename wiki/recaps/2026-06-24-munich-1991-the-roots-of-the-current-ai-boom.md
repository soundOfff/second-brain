# Recap — Munich 1991: the Roots of the Current AI Boom

An AI Blog post by **[[entities/jurgen-schmidhuber]]** (with a preface by **[[entities/david-ha]]** of **[[entities/sakana-ai]]**), arguing that nearly every core building block of today's LLM boom was published within a few months in 1991 by Schmidhuber's lab at the Technical University of Munich [2026-06-24-munich-1991-the-roots-of-the-current-ai-boom]. Schmidhuber lays out an annotated timeline: an unnormalized linear **[[concepts/transformer]]** (26 March 1991, precursor to the 2017 "Attention Is All You Need" Transformer), unsupervised pre-training for deep neural networks (30 April 1991), neural network distillation (30 April 1991), deep residual learning underlying both **[[concepts/lstm]]** and ResNets (15 June 1991), and early groundwork for generative adversarial networks via "artificial curiosity" (31 August 1991) [2026-06-24-munich-1991-the-roots-of-the-current-ai-boom].

The piece is explicitly a priority claim: Schmidhuber argues these 1991 papers anticipated the "T" (Transformer) and "P" (pre-training) in ChatGPT, and that neural distillation is "central to" DeepSeek's 2025 R1 release [2026-06-24-munich-1991-the-roots-of-the-current-ai-boom]. He also claims the two most-cited scientific papers of all time (as of January 2026) both derive from this 1991 body of work [2026-06-24-munich-1991-the-roots-of-the-current-ai-boom]. Note this is a self-published, self-authored historical/priority argument by the researcher whose own work is being credited — it should be read as Schmidhuber's contention about credit and lineage, not a neutral third-party historical account.

## Key claims

- Schmidhuber's TU Munich lab published, within roughly March–August 1991: the first Transformer variant ("unnormalized linear Transformer"), unsupervised pre-training for deep nets, neural network distillation, deep residual learning, and foundational GAN concepts [2026-06-24-munich-1991-the-roots-of-the-current-ai-boom].
- The unnormalized linear Transformer (26 March 1991) is claimed as predecessor to the 2017 "normalized quadratic Transformer" from "Attention Is All You Need," which the author says did not cite the 1991 work [2026-06-24-munich-1991-the-roots-of-the-current-ai-boom].
- Deep residual learning (15 June 1991, via Sepp Hochreiter's diploma thesis) is described as the core ingredient of both LSTM (most-cited AI paper of the 20th century) and a Highway-Net-derived ResNet variant (claimed most-cited paper of the 21st century) [2026-06-24-munich-1991-the-roots-of-the-current-ai-boom].
- Neural network distillation from 1991 is claimed as key to DeepSeek's 2025 "R1"/"Sputnik" moment [2026-06-24-munich-1991-the-roots-of-the-current-ai-boom].
- The author (Schmidhuber) argues 1991 work already showed LLM-like neural nets alone are insufficient for AGI, motivating parallel research into world models, planning, and recursive self-improvement since the late 1980s [2026-06-24-munich-1991-the-roots-of-the-current-ai-boom].
- David Ha's preface credits this lineage as having shaped his own work on World Models (2018) and current recursive self-improvement research at Sakana AI [2026-06-24-munich-1991-the-roots-of-the-current-ai-boom].
- Munich was also home to the first self-driving cars in traffic (Ernst Dickmanns's team, reaching up to 175 km/h), which the author cites as further evidence Munich was an AI epicenter before commercial AI shifted to the Pacific Rim [2026-06-24-munich-1991-the-roots-of-the-current-ai-boom].

# Recap — Power to the People: How LLMs Flip the Script on Technology Diffusion

A blog post by **[[entities/andrej-karpathy]]** (originally dated 07 Apr 2025, captured
2026-07-25) argues that LLMs invert the usual top-down pattern of technology diffusion
— historically new technologies (electricity, cryptography, computers, flight, the
internet, GPS) start in government/military contexts, move through corporations, and
reach individuals last — because LLMs instead deliver disproportionate benefit to
regular individuals first, while adoption in corporations and governments lags
[2026-07-25-power-to-the-people-how-llms-flip-the-script-on-technology-d]. He cites
ChatGPT as the fastest-growing consumer application in history, at 400 million weekly
active users, as evidence [2026-07-25-power-to-the-people-how-llms-flip-the-script-on-technology-d].

Karpathy's explanation (his own argument, not a cited empirical finding): LLMs offer
"quasi-expert" competence spread broadly across many domains, which is transformative
for an individual (who is normally expert in at most one thing, so an LLM lets them do
things they couldn't do before — vibe-code apps, parse legal documents, read research
papers, do data analytics) but only marginally improves an organization, which already
concentrates deep, narrow expertise across hired specialists
[2026-07-25-power-to-the-people-how-llms-flip-the-script-on-technology-d]. He adds two
further reasons for corporate/government lag: organizations face far greater
complexity, coordination needs, compliance and error-cost constraints that don't fit
neatly into a context window, and they suffer structural inertia (culture, bureaucracy,
political turf wars, retraining overhead) that resists rapid adoption of a
versatile-but-shallow-and-fallible tool [2026-07-25-power-to-the-people-how-llms-flip-the-script-on-technology-d].

Looking forward, Karpathy speculates that this democratized state depends on frontier
LLM capability currently being cheap/accessible such that "money can't buy a better
ChatGPT" (he notes Bill Gates uses the same GPT-4o as anyone) — but flags that
train-time scaling, test-time scaling, and model ensembles could restore a
capital-driven performance gap, even as distillation pushes the other way; if that
happens, he predicts large organizations and wealthy individuals could pull back ahead
[2026-07-25-power-to-the-people-how-llms-flip-the-script-on-technology-d]. He closes by
riffing on William Gibson's "the future is already here, it's just not evenly
distributed" — arguing the AI revolution instead arrived unusually evenly distributed,
which he says he personally welcomes [2026-07-25-power-to-the-people-how-llms-flip-the-script-on-technology-d].

## Key claims

- ChatGPT is described as the fastest-growing consumer application in history, with 400
  million weekly active users [2026-07-25-power-to-the-people-how-llms-flip-the-script-on-technology-d].
- Karpathy's central argument: LLMs disproportionately empower individuals over
  organizations because they provide broad quasi-expert capability, which is
  transformative for a single-domain-expert individual but only incrementally improves
  an organization that already aggregates many specialists (his opinion)
  [2026-07-25-power-to-the-people-how-llms-flip-the-script-on-technology-d].
- He argues organizational adoption lags due to complexity/compliance/coordination
  overhead and structural inertia (culture, bureaucracy, retraining costs) (his opinion)
  [2026-07-25-power-to-the-people-how-llms-flip-the-script-on-technology-d].
- He notes frontier-grade LLM performance is currently cheap and not meaningfully
  improvable by spending more money, but flags train-time scaling, test-time scaling,
  and model ensembles as forces that could reintroduce a capital-driven capability gap
  (his speculation) [2026-07-25-power-to-the-people-how-llms-flip-the-script-on-technology-d].
- He warns that if such a gap reopens, the "individual" category itself could split,
  with wealthier people's children tutored by more capable models than others'
  (his speculation) [2026-07-25-power-to-the-people-how-llms-flip-the-script-on-technology-d].

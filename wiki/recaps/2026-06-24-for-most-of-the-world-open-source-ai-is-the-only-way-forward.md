# Recap — For Most of the World, Open-Source AI Is the Only Way Forward

A report (by Steven Vaughan-Nichols for Techstrong AI) on a keynote by [[entities/yann-lecun]] at United Nations Open Source Week in NYC, in which LeCun — formerly Meta's chief AI scientist — argued that open-source AI is not optional but the only viable path to AI sovereignty, cultural/linguistic diversity, and long-term safety [2026-06-24-for-most-of-the-world-open-source-ai-is-the-only-way-forward]. His central claim (opinion) is that most countries lack the resources to build frontier models alone, but could collectively out-build proprietary systems via shared open platforms; national delegates from Morocco, Sierra Leone, and Jamaica reportedly agreed, as did Alberto Gago of Spain's AESIA, who called for a "transparent, equitable, and human" AI ecosystem [2026-06-24-for-most-of-the-world-open-source-ai-is-the-only-way-forward].

LeCun describes his post-Meta work — the AI Alliance, Advanced Machine Intelligence Labs, and "[[concepts/project-tapestry|Project Tapestry]]" — as a concrete instantiation of this vision: a bottom-up, GitHub-based confederation where participants train a shared global model by exchanging parameter vectors rather than sharing raw data, preserving data sovereignty [2026-06-24-for-most-of-the-world-open-source-ai-is-the-only-way-forward]. He cited early participation from European countries, Switzerland, the UK, the UAE, India, Kazakhstan, Vietnam, Japan, and Korea, plus IBM, NVIDIA, AMD, and Intel, and said (opinion/hope) Tapestry could be in production by early 2027 [2026-06-24-for-most-of-the-world-open-source-ai-is-the-only-way-forward].

LeCun draws a historical analogy to proprietary Unix/hardware stacks being displaced by commodity hardware plus open-source software in the early 2000s, and argues AI is following the same "inevitable" trajectory [2026-06-24-for-most-of-the-world-open-source-ai-is-the-only-way-forward]. He pushes back hard (opinion) on existential-risk arguments for restricting open models, comparing such restriction to "medieval obscurantism" akin to limiting the printing press, and specifically argues bioweapon risk from AI access is overstated (recipe access isn't the bottleneck; building one is) and that AI-driven cybersecurity risk is offset by AI-driven defense [2026-06-24-for-most-of-the-world-open-source-ai-is-the-only-way-forward]. Separately, he argues current closed-model economics are unsustainable, citing a claim that OpenAI's $200/month professional subscription costs roughly $15,000/month per power user to serve — subsidized today by investors — implying prices must rise or inference costs must fall sharply (or both) [2026-06-24-for-most-of-the-world-open-source-ai-is-the-only-way-forward]. He also describes smart-glasses-based crop-disease-query experiments with farmers in India as an example of value achievable with small, locally-run open models once inference costs drop by a factor of 20–100 [2026-06-24-for-most-of-the-world-open-source-ai-is-the-only-way-forward].

## Key claims

- LeCun: most countries cannot afford to build frontier-scale LLMs alone, but a collaborative open-source effort could surpass proprietary systems [2026-06-24-for-most-of-the-world-open-source-ai-is-the-only-way-forward].
- Project Tapestry trains a global model via federated exchange of parameter vectors, not raw data, and is open/bottom-up (sign up via GitHub, no authorization gate) [2026-06-24-for-most-of-the-world-open-source-ai-is-the-only-way-forward].
- LeCun hopes Tapestry reaches production by early 2027 [2026-06-24-for-most-of-the-world-open-source-ai-is-the-only-way-forward].
- LeCun (opinion): AI existential/security risk arguments are "very, very widely overstated," and restricting open-source AI access on those grounds is dangerous for democracy and culture [2026-06-24-for-most-of-the-world-open-source-ai-is-the-only-way-forward].
- LeCun cites a figure that a $200/month AI subscription costs the provider about $15,000/month to serve per power user, calling current pricing unsustainable without investor subsidy [2026-06-24-for-most-of-the-world-open-source-ai-is-the-only-way-forward].
- LeCun: inference costs need to fall by a factor of 20–100 for many high-value small-model applications (e.g., crop-disease diagnosis via smart glasses for Indian farmers) to become broadly viable [2026-06-24-for-most-of-the-world-open-source-ai-is-the-only-way-forward].
